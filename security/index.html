Leaser connects intelligence and coordinates action to continuously improve NOI and enterprise asset value. That responsibility calls for clear boundaries: how data is used, who can act, and how decisions are understood.

Our approach brings permissions, oversight, and accountability into the way Leaser operates—whether your team runs the platform or works alongside Leaser experts.

AUTHORITY & OVERSIGHT

You set the direction. And the boundaries.

A platform that understands what to do also needs clear authority to do it. Leaser’s governance framework separates observation, recommendations, approvals, and authorized execution.

The level of authority depends on the action, the connected system, and the permissions agreed with your organization.

  1. 01

    Observe

    Monitor signals and performance without changing connected systems.

  2. 02

    Recommend

    Evaluate the situation and propose the next move for your team to consider.

  3. 03

    Approve

    Require an authorized person to review a proposed action before execution.

  4. 04

    Act within scope

    Carry out supported actions within expressly approved limits.

Access to information does not grant permission to act.
EXPLAINABLE INTELLIGENCE

Good intelligence can show its work.

When a recommendation affects demand, leasing, or revenue, your team needs to understand the reasoning behind it.

Leaser’s decision framework connects the proposed move to the signals that informed it, the alternatives considered, the expected economic effect, and the uncertainty that remains. The goal is to make decisions easier to evaluate, challenge, and improve.

01

The evidence

Which market, demand, prospect, leasing, or performance signals informed the recommendation?

02

The trade-offs

What alternatives were considered, and what could change the recommendation?

03

The expected effect

How could the action affect leasing performance, cost, revenue, and NOI?

04

The confidence

Where is the evidence strong, and where does uncertainty require more review?

A forecast is an estimate. An observed result is evidence. The distinction matters.
DATA RIGHTS & USE

Your data has a purpose. And boundaries.

Connected intelligence depends on relevant information. The purpose of each connection—and the permission behind it—should be clear.

Our data approach starts with approved sources, defined uses, and respect for the information entrusted to Leaser.

Customer data stays yours

You retain ownership of the customer data you provide. Its use is governed by your agreement with Leaser.

Access serves an agreed purpose

Data sources, permitted fields, and access levels are defined around the work Leaser is authorized to perform.

Confidential information stays protected

Customer data is not sold. Access by personnel and service providers is limited to an authorized need and confidentiality obligations.

Learning has defined limits

Any use of deidentified or aggregated information for model improvement must follow the agreed data terms. Using identifiable prospect or resident information to train a generalized model for unrelated customers requires express written authorization.

SECURITY & ACCESS

Access should follow responsibility.

Ownership teams, operators, marketers, and Leaser specialists have different responsibilities. Access should reflect the work each person is authorized to do.

Our security approach centers on individual authentication, access limited to the task, secure data transmission, and confidentiality obligations. Permissions to view information and permissions to change a connected system are treated separately.

During evaluation, we work through your requirements for user access, data handling, third-party services, retention, and incident response. The applicable controls and responsibilities are documented for your engagement.

ECONOMIC ACCOUNTABILITY

Accountability reaches all the way to NOI.

A cheaper lead can still be an expensive lease. A fuller building can still produce less income. Meaningful oversight asks what a decision does to the economics of the asset.

Leaser’s governance framework considers the relationship between demand, conversion, occupancy, revenue, and cost. The objective is to continuously improve NOI and enterprise asset value, with visibility into the risks and trade-offs along the way.

The accountability model follows the full loop: what the system understood, what it simulated, what was decided, what action was authorized, and what the results taught us.

PRACTICAL QUESTIONS

Clear answers before you connect.

Does connecting a system allow Leaser to change it?

A data connection and execution authority are separate permissions. Supported changes require the appropriate access and the authority agreed with your organization.

Who remains responsible for strategy and approvals?

Your organization defines its objectives, policies, and authorized decision-makers. Leaser provides intelligence and execution within the agreed scope, whether your team operates it directly or works with Leaser experts.

Can our IT and security teams review Leaser before onboarding?

Yes. Bring them into the conversation. We’ll review the proposed connections, data use, access requirements, and applicable controls with your team before defining the engagement.
